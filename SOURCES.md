# Source audit and application evidence

Search and inspection date: **22 August 2026**. Links below point to publisher or primary research records where available.

## Mathematical lineage

1. Agent collective and I. Pitchford. *Exact two-item resource-constrained joint replenishment and its independent-cap relaxation gap*, v1.2.0-candidate. Evidence Press / Zenodo, 2026. [Release page](https://evidencepress.org/releases/certified-two-item-jrp/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21855894) · [repository](https://github.com/ipitchford/certified-two-item-jrp). This reproduces the canonical predecessor-page citation metadata. Used for the producer-side unrefereed two-item interval/upper endpoint, model definition, resource-to-cap reduction, and assurance boundary; not an externally established exact constant.
2. D. Segev. *Resource-constrained joint replenishment via power-of-\(m^{1/k}\) policies*. arXiv:2603.18720v1, 2026. [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18720). Used for the reported \(5/(6\ln 2)\) upper comparator and resource-constrained model comparison. This is a preprint, distinct from Segev's peer-reviewed 2026 *Mathematics of Operations Research* article.
3. A. S. Schulz and C. Telha. “Integer factorization: Why two-item joint replenishment is hard.” *Operations Research* 72(3), 1192–1202, 2024. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2022.2390). Used for the arithmetic-complexity context of the exact two-item problem.
4. D. Segev. “The continuous-time joint replenishment problem: \(\varepsilon\)-optimal policies via pairwise alignment.” *Management Science* 71(5), 4183–4197, 2025. [Publisher record and correct DOI](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2023.00705). Used for pairwise-alignment context. The supplied source file incorrectly gave DOI suffix `02046`; the publisher record establishes `00705`.
5. D. Segev. “Improved approximation guarantees for joint replenishment in continuous time.” *Mathematics of Operations Research* 51(2), 1683–1712, 2026. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.2024.0635). Peer-reviewed unconstrained approximation context; not the source of the resource-constrained \(5/(6\ln2)\) comparator.
6. R. Roundy. “Rounding off to powers of two in continuous relaxations of capacitated lot sizing problems.” *Management Science* 35(12), 1433–1442, 1989. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.35.12.1433). Classical rounding/relaxation context.
7. C.-P. Teo and D. Bertsimas. “Multistage lot sizing problems via randomized rounding.” *Operations Research* 49(4), 599–608, 2001. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.49.4.599.11222). Randomized-rounding and fixed-base JRP context.
8. T. Cohen-Hillel and L. Yedidsion. “The periodic joint replenishment problem is strongly NP-hard.” *Mathematics of Operations Research* 43(4), 1269–1289, 2018. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.2017.0904). Complexity context.
9. S. Creemers and T. Stephan. *Special Cases of the Joint Replenishment Problem (JRP): A Mathematical Analysis*. SSRN 6674418, posted 29 April 2026 and revised 27 May 2026. [SSRN record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6674418). The preprint exactly characterises can-order policies when one of holding, major-order, or minor-order cost is removed, over finite and infinite horizons. Its can-order inventory-policy object and zero-cost special cases are adjacent but distinct from the deterministic common-origin fixed-interval independent-cap gap oracle here; it neither supplies nor is contradicted by the present triple-tail construction.

The targeted search found no source that supplied the same second-smallest-pair-complexity triple tail or the bundled exact rational lower bound. That absence is not proof of novelty or priority.

## Priority-search protocol

- **Cutoff and date:** searches run through 22 August 2026; results after that date are outside the audit.
- **Authoritative routes:** DOI content negotiation/Crossref metadata, INFORMS PubsOnLine, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, the South African Journal of Industrial Engineering, arXiv, SSRN, and the public predecessor record.
- **Object-level queries:** `"second-smallest pair complexity" joint replenishment`; `"three-item" "joint replenishment" exact oracle`; `joint replenishment independent frequency caps integrality gap`; `joint replenishment commensurable periods union density exact three items`; plus title/author/DOI searches for every bibliography record.
- **Equivalent-form queries:** common-origin/fixed-interval/periodic policies; integer-multiplier and indirect-grouping policies; union/meeting density; cap/average-rate/resource-constrained relaxations; exact evaluation; lower-bound and integrality-gap terminology.
- **Tracing:** backward references from Peng et al., Roundy, Teo–Bertsimas, Cohen-Hillel–Yedidsion, Creemers–Boute, Creemers–Stephan, and the two Segev lines; forward/citing-record scans where publisher/index routes exposed them; comparison against the predecessor bibliography and all five internal reviewer reports.
- **Exclusions:** stochastic can-order policies, discrete deadline JRP, dynamic lot sizing, general joint-cost JRP, and warehouse-retailer deadline models were recorded as adjacent but not priority matches unless they shared the same deterministic common-origin fixed-interval object and cap relaxation.
- **Result:** no located record supplied this second-smallest-pair-complexity finite generator, its triple tail, and the same rational witness. This calibrated absence is not proof of novelty or priority; the claim remains “targeted search found no match.”

## Evidence-use boundary

The external cases show that real logistics decisions involve service, uncertainty, minimum quantities, transport nonlinearities, capacity, and computational trade-offs. They motivate the staged evaluation protocol; they do not validate the three-item certificate, establish transfer, or license any claim of savings.
